## (b) Direct answers to the three questions

### Q1. Is "switch from vision-based control in free space to tactile-based control once contact is established" already a well-established, published recipe? By whom, and with what kind of hand/sensor?

**Short answer: Yes — it is well established at three distinct levels, but it is not usually implemented as a single hard switch inside one learned policy.** The levels:

1. **Classical robotics (decades old):** hybrid force/position control with visual servoing for approach and force/compliance control for insertion is the textbook recipe for assembly (Lozano-Pérez; Zhang & Chen tactile Jacobian 2000; Li et al. 2013; Kappassov et al. 2020 — all described in ✅ #2/#3/#4). ✅ #16 (*From a Single Demonstration*, 2026) still uses exactly this decomposition: a *free-space primitive* driven by SIFT visual servoing and *in-contact* plane/translation/rotation primitives driven by compliance, with transitions triggered by **contact-making / contact-breaking events detected from force thresholds**. Sensor: wrist F/T, parallel gripper, single arm.

2. **Explicit modality switching inside a learned policy — the literal recipe:**
   - ✅ **CAAT** (arXiv:2608.01102, 2026) is the cleanest published instance I found. It trains a **contact classifier on tactile-image difference** (`|T_t − T_ref|`) to output a binary contact phase, then uses that phase to scale vision vs tactile attention readouts: *"emphasises visual information before contact and tactile information during contact."* Embodiment: **two-finger parallel gripper** (visuo-tactile UMI device), tactile image tokens, ACT/DP/π0 backbones, real + UniVTAC sim. Caveat I must flag: in their own Table 1, the **binary** contact-gated variant (51.8%) *underperformed* a numerical scaling variant (69.6%) and even a gated baseline (59.6%) — so the naive binary switch is not automatically best.
   - ✅ **Kamijo et al.** (arXiv:2309.15681) do a **dual-policy switch**: force-controlled RL insertion policy by default; when tactile-derived tilt exceeds a threshold, a tactile active-inference alignment policy takes over. Sensor: **GelSight Mini** on a **2F-85 parallel gripper**, real UR5e. This is contact-triggered policy switching with touch as the trigger.
   - ✅ **MS-Bot / Play to the Score** (arXiv:2408.01366): stage-guided dynamic fusion where **vision dominates the aligning (free-space) stage and touch dominates rotation/insertion stages**; parallel gripper + GelSight Mini; real. The stage is predicted from observation + action history rather than from touch, but the *effect* is the vision→touch handover.
   - ✅ **RDP** (arXiv:2503.02881): a different flavour — a slow visual/diffusion policy and a **fast tactile/force controller that runs continuously**. Not a discrete switch, but it is the "vision plans, touch corrects" recipe; parallel grippers, GelSight Mini/MCTac/joint torque; real. Notably, *observation-level* tactile fusion (DP + tactile embedding) did **not** beat DP, which is evidence that *how* touch is used matters more than whether it is present.

3. **"Touch is only informative during contact" as an explicit design prior:** ✅ CAAT states this as its central motivation and cites the phase-dependence of tactile utility. ✅ StageACT uses joint-torque spikes only to *annotate* contact transitions (not as policy input).

**So:** the recipe is established, mostly with **parallel two-finger grippers and GelSight/DIGIT-style or force/torque sensing, in real robots**. What is *less* standard is a single learned architecture with an explicit, tactile-triggered hard switch — CAAT and Kamijo are the closest, and CAAT's own ablations show the binary version is not a slam dunk.

### Q2. Has anyone done this for BIMANUAL multi-finger dexterous hands in SIMULATION across many different hand morphologies?

**Short answer: The infrastructure now exists; I did not find the specific recipe (vision→tactile contact-triggered switching) published for that setting. Treat this as largely open, with one important 2026 enabler.**

Evidence:

- ✅ **Bench2Dex** (arXiv:2609.15726, Sep 2026) is a **simulation benchmark for visuo-tactile bimanual manipulation across 12 dexterous hands**, with a *unified simulated tactile interface* that ray-casts local contact geometry along each hand mesh's surface normals into image-like tactile maps (building on TacMap's geometry-consistent penetration-depth encoding). It has 26 long-horizon tasks, ~1.3K human-teleoperated demos, 8 synchronized modalities, and a latched stage-completion metric. It explicitly claims: *"to our knowledge, no existing benchmark supports visuo-tactile data collection across multiple dexterous hands under one setting."* It benchmarks ACT, DP, π0.5 and GR00T N1.5. **However, Bench2Dex is a benchmark — it does not propose a contact-triggered vision→tactile controller switch.**
- Adjacent benchmarks it contrasts against: Bi-DexHands (dual Shadow Hands, RL, **no vision**), DexMimicGen (3 dexterous embodiments, 9 tasks, **no tactile**), DexJoCo (2 embodiments, **no tactile**), DexVerse (1 embodiment, **no tactile**), RoboMIND 2.0 (vision-based tactile but **parallel-jaw grippers across 6 embodiments**). So before Bench2Dex, the combination {bimanual × multi-finger dexterous × tactile × many morphologies × simulation} was not jointly covered.
- ✅ **UniVTAC** (arXiv:2602.10093) is a visuo-tactile sim platform (Isaac Sim + TacEx, 3 sensor models) but with a **parallel gripper** (both fingertips), 8 tasks.
- ✅ **FTP-1** (arXiv:2606.13102) reaches dexterous hands and cross-sensor transfer, but its cross-sensor evaluation is on **real** setups (Sharpa North, Sharpa&Dexmate, FlexivXense, TactileUMI), not many morphologies in simulation.
- ✅ **FTP-1** and **Bench2Dex** both cite TACTO/Taxim as image-based tactile simulation lineage; ✅ the survey (#17) covers the simulation-platform landscape.

**Conclusion with uncertainty:** I found **no published work** that (i) is simulation, (ii) bimanual, (iii) multi-finger dexterous, (iv) across many hand morphologies, and (v) implements a contact-phase-triggered handover from vision-based to tactile-based control. **Bench2Dex (2026) is the benchmark that makes this study possible**, and it explicitly does not itself do the switching. This looks like a genuine gap — but note my search was bounded by what was fetchable, and IEEE-only papers (e.g., HIVE-6D/ViTaDex-adjacent dexterous in-hand work) were not readable, so a negative claim is not airtight.

### Q3. Is there published work using tactile-derived contact phase to select among several learned policy heads for long-horizon staged tasks?

**Short answer: Not exactly. All the pieces exist separately, but I did not find the precise combination (tactile-derived phase → routing among multiple learned policy heads for long-horizon staged tasks). This is the most defensible "open" answer of the three.**

What exists, decomposed:

| Piece | Work | Is the phase *tactile-derived*? | Does it *select among policy heads*? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Policy-head routing conditioned on state/phase | ✅ BRIDGE (#6) — base vs support diffusion heads via MLP router + hysteresis | ❌ vision+state | ✅ yes (2 heads, hard switch) |
| Phase-specific action paths/experts, supervised phase | ✅ TRACT (#7) — phase-specific query banks + action residuals; ✅ MoE-ACT (#8) — H phase experts + supervised gate | ❌ vision+proprio / vision-only | ✅ yes (routed paths / experts) |
| Stage-conditioned single policy | ✅ StageACT (#9) — one ACT conditioned on one-hot stage | ❌ stages annotated from visual+torque spikes | ❌ single policy, not head selection |
| Stage-guided multi-sensory fusion | ✅ MS-Bot (#10) — stage token → cross-attention reweights modality features | ❌ phase from obs+action history | ❌ modality weights, not separate policy heads |
| Contact-phase-gated *modality* scaling | ✅ CAAT (#11) — binary **tactile contact classifier** → re-scale vision vs tactile readouts | ✅ **yes, tactile-derived** | ⚠️ partial — it gates modality readouts, not multiple learned policy heads |
| Contact-triggered switch between two policies | ✅ Kamijo (#1) — tactile tilt > threshold → hand over from RL insertion policy to active-inference alignment policy | ✅ **yes, tactile-derived** | ⚠️ partial — two policies, but it's a 2-way handover, not a multi-head long-horizon router |

**Conclusion:** The mechanism **"tactile contact phase → gate between learned policies"** has not (to my finding) been published as a general multi-head router for long-horizon staged tasks. The nearest works are **CAAT** (tactile contact classifier gating modality attention inside one policy) and **Kamijo** (tactile tilt thresholding a dual-policy switch). The multi-head phase routers (**BRIDGE, TRACT, MoE-ACT**) do not use touch. **Uncertainty:** this is an absence-of-evidence claim from a bounded set of fetchable sources; it is plausible that an IEEE/CoRL paper does this and I could not reach it.

## (c) What is standard/classical vs what is still open

### Standard / classical (settled)

- **Image-based vs pose-based tactile servoing (IBTS/PBTS)** is a formalised control paradigm, explicitly built as the tactile analogue of visual servoing (visual-servoing taxonomy: image-based vs pose-based vs hybrid). Established by Zhang & Chen (tactile Jacobian), Li et al., Kappassov et al., and modernised with deep pose estimation by Lepora & Lloyd. ✅ #2/#3/#4.
- **Tactile pose + shear estimation with uncertainty, SE(3) Bayesian filtering, and velocity-based servo control** is a mature, reproducible stack (TacTip + GDN + Lie-group filter). ✅ #3/#4.
- **Hybrid force/position control + compliant in-contact primitives, with contact-making/contact-breaking transition events**, is the classical assembly recipe and still competitive in 2026. ✅ #16.
- **Force/torque and joint-torque contact detection** (momentum observers, residual thresholds) is standard and cheap. ✅ #18.
- **In contact-rich imitation learning, action chunking + a fast tactile/force correction loop** (slow-fast) is becoming standard: ✅ #5 (RDP). And the negative result "just concatenating tactile tokens does not reliably help" is now repeatedly observed (RDP; FTP-1 reporting π0.5 outperforming two tactile baselines; CAAT's motivation).
- **Phase/stage conditioning of imitation policies** is established as a robustness fix for partial observability and mode collapse: ✅ #9 (StageACT), ✅ #7 (TRACT), ✅ #8 (MoE-ACT), ✅ #10 (MS-Bot).
- **Cross-sensor tactile representation unification and large-scale tactile pretraining** is now established (FTP-1 with 21 sensors; UniVTAC encoders). ✅ #13/#12.

### Still open (as of this survey)

1. **Tactile-derived contact phase as the router for multiple learned policy heads** — the exact Q3 combination. Nearest: CAAT, Kamijo. **Open.**
2. **The vision→tactile handover for bimanual multi-finger dexterous hands in simulation across many morphologies** — Bench2Dex supplies the benchmark but not the method. **Open.**
3. **Whether a *hard binary* contact switch beats *continuous* modulation.** CAAT's own numbers show binary contact gating (51.8%) losing to numerical scaling (69.6%) and to a gated baseline (59.6%). **Open and empirically contested.**
4. **Contact-phase estimation that is robust and tactile-only for long-horizon procedural tasks** — most phase predictors are vision/proprioception-based; tactile is usually only a binary contact classifier (CAAT) or a tilt threshold (Kamijo). **Open.**
5. **Simulated tactile fidelity vs real sensors for dexterous hands** — Bench2Dex explicitly declines to claim its simulated tactile maps reproduce any physical sensor; FTP-1 shows a large sim/tactile transfer gap. **Open.**
6. **Slip detection and grip-force regulation as a first-class learned controller** (not just observation) — I could only see this via search metadata and the survey's "Slip and Contact Event Monitoring" section (#17); the specific slip papers were not fetchable. **Likely partially settled but I could not verify the primary sources.**
7. **Full-body / whole-arm contact state** (not just fingertips) for free-space↔contact switching — #18 is a rare example that detects contact anywhere on the arm from joint torques. **Open.**

## (d) Most important papers to read in full (my ranked shortlist)

1. **CAAT — arXiv:2608.01102** ✅ https://arxiv.org/html/2608.01102
   *Why:* The single most direct answer to Q1/Q3: tactile-image-based contact classifier driving vision-vs-tactile attention scaling. Read the ablation table carefully (binary vs learnable vs numerical gating) — it is the strongest existing evidence about how to do the handover.

2. **Bench2Dex — arXiv:2609.15726** ✅ https://arxiv.org/html/2609.15726
   *Why:* The enabling benchmark for Q2 (12 bimanual dexterous hands, simulated image-like tactile, 26 multi-stage tasks, stage-level metric). Read the unified tactile acquisition appendix and the policy results to see what current visuo-tactile policies do and do not use touch for.

3. **Kamijo et al., Tactile-based Active Inference — arXiv:2309.15681** ✅ https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2309.15681
   *Why:* A working, real-robot **tactile-triggered dual-policy switch** with GelSight Mini on a parallel gripper, with strong numbers (90% at <0.1 mm). The template for "touch decides when to change policy."

4. **TRACT — arXiv:2607.29285** ✅ https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2607.29285 and **BRIDGE — arXiv:2606.26603** ✅ https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26603
   *Why:* The two most sophisticated phase-routing policy architectures (intra-chunk phase boundaries; state-gated diffusion experts). Neither uses touch — which is exactly the gap you could fill. Read for the routing mechanism and the failure modes they fix (phase ambiguity, stalls, mode averaging).

5. **RDP — arXiv:2503.02881** ✅ https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2503.02881
   *Why:* The reference slow-fast visual-tactile control paper, plus the important negative result that observation-level tactile fusion is not enough. Good baseline design for any "tactile local control" claim.

6. **Lepora & Lloyd, Pose-Based Tactile Servoing (arXiv:2012.02504)** ✅ https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2012.02504 and **Pose and shear-based tactile servoing (arXiv:2312.08411)** ✅ https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2312.08411
   *Why:* The canonical modern tactile-servoing formalism (IBTS/PBTS, tactile pose+shear, SE(3) filtering, dual-arm). Read if you need the control-theoretic foundation rather than a learned policy.

7. **From a Single Demonstration to a General Policy — arXiv:2605.17601** ✅ https://arxiv.org/html/2605.17601
   *Why:* The cleanest, most legible statement of the free-space (visual servoing) / in-contact (compliant EC primitives) decomposition with explicit contact-event transitions, over 7 real multi-stage tasks at >90%.

8. **MS-Bot / Play to the Score — arXiv:2408.01366** ✅ https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2408.01366
   *Why:* Stage-guided modality fusion with interpretable per-stage modality attention; the peg-insertion-with-keyway task is exactly a tactile-critical staged task. Also a strong "phase conditions fusion" precedent.

9. **FTP-1 — arXiv:2606.13102** ✅ https://arxiv.org/html/2606.13102 and **UniVTAC — arXiv:2602.10093** ✅ https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2602.10093
   *Why:* Read together for the representation/simulation layers: cross-sensor tactile token unification, and a visuo-tactile sim benchmark + encoder. Important for the simulation-across-morphologies question (Q2).

10. **Survey: Vision-Based Tactile Intelligence for Robotics — arXiv:2608.15490** ✅ https://arxiv.org/html/2608.15490v1
    *Why:* Use as a map and citation mine, especially §IV-B3 (slip/contact events), §IV-F (task-oriented tactile learning), §V (simulation platforms), §VI (open challenges).

**Also worth obtaining if you have IEEE/Nature access (I could not):** HIVE-6D (https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/11543317), ViTaDex (https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11330173), "A staged vision-force collaborative framework for precision robotic insertion of metallic valve components" (Nature Sci. Rep. s41598-026-57025-z — snippet explicitly mentions "Contact-state recognition and mode switching" with four states: non-contact, initial contact, …), and the Center-of-Pressure tactile servoing paper (https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/11543144).
