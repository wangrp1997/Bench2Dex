# Cross-Embodiment / Cross-Hand Transfer of Tactile Sensing for Manipulation

**Literature survey — compiled 2026 (survey window: 2024 → Sep 2026 arXiv)**
All claims below come from pages I actually fetched (arXiv HTML via `arxiv.org/html/<id>`, `ar5iv`, or `ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org`). Where I relied on a secondary source or could not open the primary paper, I say so explicitly. Nothing here is invented.

---

## 0. The one question

> **Has anyone demonstrated a TACTILE-CONDITIONED manipulation policy trained on one set of dexterous hands that transfers (zero-shot or few-shot) to a DIFFERENT dexterous hand morphology with different finger structure and different tactile sensor layout — especially in SIMULATION across many hands?**

**Short answer: No — not as of the material I could find (through ~Sep 2026).** The capability is unclaimed, and the three nearest-miss works each say so themselves (see §2).

## (b) Direct answer to the question, with the evidence

### Answer: **No work I could find demonstrates it.** Confidence: high (~85%) for the negative, with one unresolved lead (§Caveat).

The claim decomposes into four requirements. Here is where each stands:

**Requirement 1 — zero-shot/few-shot transfer to a *different dexterous hand morphology* (not just a different sensor or a human–robot gap).**
- ✅ Achieved **without touch**: CrossDex (4→2), DexGrasp-Zero (4→2, RSS 2026), XL-VLA (4 hands, latent action space), UHAS (4 hands, zero-shot setting).
- ⚠️ Achieved **once with calibrated contact** (not tactile): MARC / *Transferring Contact, Not Just Motion* — zero-shot to unseen **Wuji 15**, but (i) contact = actuator effort calibrated via **per-hand system identification**, not a tactile array/skin; (ii) Wuji 15 is a **DoF variant of a hand family already in training**, not a different finger structure; (iii) real robot, not simulation.
- ❌ **Never with a tactile sensor.**

**Requirement 2 — *different tactile sensor layout* across the transfer boundary.**
- ✅ Achieved as a **sensor**-transfer problem: FTP-1 (unseen sensor setups, +31.6%), T3 (zero-shot to GelSight Svelte / DenseTact 2.0), UniTacHand (glove → Inspire array), Bench2Dex (12 different tactile layouts under one representation).
- ❌ But in these works the *hand morpology* is either fixed (UniTacHand, T3, TacMap, canonical 3D tactile) or the transfer is resolved by **training a new per-sensor encoder and finetuning per setup** (FTP-1). FTP-1's own ablation shows the gain comes from pretrained tactile knowledge, but the mechanism is "encoder re-init + finetune," not morphology-invariant inference.

**Requirement 3 — in simulation across many hands.**
- ✅ Bench2Dex has exactly the infrastructure: **12 hands, unified 240×240 surface-aligned tactile maps, 8–10 sites per hand, ray-cast penetration depth, open-sourced.** This is the single most important artifact for the question.
- ❌ Bench2Dex does **not** run the experiment. Its own words: policies are evaluated "using RGB and proprioceptive observations"; "tasks and embodiments are not factorially crossed"; and the framework is offered for *"extending evaluation to tactile-conditioned policies and transfer across dexterous hands."*
- ⚠️ roto 2.0 (4 morphologies, tactile-only RL) trains **separate policies per morphology** — a benchmark for convergence, not a transfer result.
- ⚠️ The IEEE Access dex-peg-in-hole benchmark spans "multiple embodiments" in Isaac Gym with **both wrist force/torque and finger tactile**, and is the reading of the question that best matches "simulation across many hands with touch" — but I could not read the primary paper, and the available summary does not claim cross-hand transfer (it presents a benchmark + a closed-loop pose estimator at 49.68%).

**Requirement 4 — tactile-conditioned *manipulation policy* (not just a representation).**
- ✅ Tactile-conditioned policies exist and are strong: TactAlign, UniTacHand, FTP-1, TTP, UVTA, MARC, TactiDex, TacBPM.
- ❌ Every one of them is human→robot or single-robot-hand. The two that use multiple *robot* hands (MARC = 3+1 real; TTP = Inspire/DexBotic/gripper) are either non-tactile (MARC uses torque) or do not report a held-out-hand zero-shot table in the text I read (TTP).

### The "tactile canonicalization" thread — real, but it stops one step short

There is a clear, convergent intellectual program of mapping per-sensor touch into a shared canonical frame:

| Canonical frame | Work | Reaches hand-A→hand-B policy transfer? |
|---|---|---|
| **MANO UV map** (2D skin atlas) | UniTacHand (glove 137-dim ↔ Inspire 1062-dim); reused by TTP with 351 taxels | No — 1 robot hand; TTP uses multiple platforms but no held-out-hand zero-shot table |
| **MANO UV / surface-aligned tactile grid** | Bench2Dex (240×240 per site × 12 hands) | Infrastructure only; transfer is future work |
| **Taxel unit-frame canonicalization** (+ sensor 6D pose + 3D force) | Wu et al. 2409.17549 | No — 1 hand (LEAP + PaXini); without it the policy scores **0%**, so it is clearly load-bearing |
| **Penetration-depth deform map** | TacMap (2602.21625) | No — 1 hand (SharpaWave); sim→real only |
| **Functional-area token space** (24 slots) | FTP-1 MTTS | No — sensor-level; new sensor ⇒ new encoder |
| **Object-centric contact maps** | CEDex; DexGEM contact-anchored retargeting | No — contact geometry, no tactile sensing; CEDex is grasp generation, not a closed-loop tactile policy |

So: **every ingredient of the answer exists in isolation** — (i) many-hand simulation with a unified tactile interface (Bench2Dex), (ii) tactile canonicalization across heterogeneous sensors (UniTacHand, Wu et al., TacMap, FTP-1 MTTS), (iii) zero-shot policy transfer to unseen hands (CrossDex, DexGrasp-Zero, UHAS, XL-VLA, MARC). **No paper joins all three.** The join is a small, well-specified experiment.

## (c) Candidate open problems

For each: statement · my confidence it is open · what would falsify "this is open."

### OP1 — A tactile-conditioned policy trained on N hands that transfers zero-shot to a held-out hand with different finger structure *and* different tactile layout (in simulation).
- **Confidence it is open: ~85%.** This is the headline gap. Bench2Dex built the exact substrate and declined to run the experiment; DexGrasp-Zero explicitly removed tactile before deployment; MARC substituted calibrated torque for tactile.
- **Falsified by:** any of (a) reading the IEEE Access DexPiHNet paper and finding a held-out-embodiment condition; (b) a Bench2Dex follow-up using its tactile stream with a train/held-out hand split; (c) any paper reporting a success rate for a tactile policy on a hand absent from training.
- **Concrete falsification experiment (~2 weeks):** on Bench2Dex, train one ACT/DP-style tactile-conditioned policy on 9 hands, evaluate zero-shot on 3 held-out hands, with a matched vision+proprioception control. If the tactile policy does not beat the control, the *interesting* version of OP1 is still open in a deeper sense (tactile layout is not the bottleneck).

### OP2 — A *sensor-layout-invariant* tactile encoder that requires **no per-sensor retraining and no per-hand system identification**.
- **Confidence it is open: ~70%.** Every cross-sensor result I read needs one of: a new encoder trained from scratch on the new sensor (FTP-1), per-hand torque calibration (MARC), a paired human–robot alignment set (UniTacHand, 10 min), or a learned image→depth translation model (TacMap). "Zero-shot" is currently always *zero-shot given some per-target calibration artifact*.
- **Falsified by:** a paper that instantiates a pretrained tactile encoder on a novel sensor layout with neither calibration data nor finetuning and reports downstream task success. FTP-1 is closest but explicitly trains the unseen-sensor encoder from scratch.
- **Why it's hard:** sensor responses are not just spatially reparameterized; they differ in physics (photometric vs piezoresistive vs capacitive), dynamic range, and noise spectra. A purely geometric canonicalization (UV map, deform map) discards the physics — which is exactly where slip/pre-slip lives.

### OP3 — Deciding *what* should be invariant vs. equivariant under a change of hand.
- **Confidence it is open: ~80%.** Bench2Dex is the only work I found that even formalizes an invariance/equivariance split for manipulation, and it does so for **scene perturbations** (lighting, texture, camera pose = invariant; object pose, table height = equivariant) — **not** for embodiment. There is no shared account of which contact quantities must be preserved when the hand changes (fingertip force? net wrench? object-centric contact map? per-finger load?). MARC answers "physical units"; CEDex answers "object-centric contact maps"; UHAS answers "sphere deformation"; XL-VLA answers "latent action". These are mutually inconsistent bets.
- **Falsified by:** a benchmark or paper that defines an embodiment-invariance/equivariance taxonomy with a measurable test, e.g. showing that two different hands producing the same object-centric contact map yields the same policy action.
- **Why it matters:** this is the conceptual prerequisite for OP1. If you get the invariant wrong, cross-hand transfer will be brittle even with perfect data.

### OP4 — Whether simulated tactile maps (the Bench2Dex style: ray-cast penetration depth on a nominal surface) are informative enough that cross-hand *transfer* results in simulation predict anything about real hardware.
- **Confidence it is open: ~75%.** Bench2Dex is candid: *"the hand and tactile models in Bench2Dex have not been calibrated against matching physical hardware"* and it "does not assume that simulated tactile observations can replace real tactile sensing." Meanwhile the real-tactile sim-to-real works (TacMap, Tac2Real, 2607.04940, TacBPM) are all single-hand. So there is **no** study linking (many-hand sim tactile transfer) → (real cross-hand tactile transfer).
- **Falsified by:** a sim-to-real study that trains a tactile policy on ≥3 hands with different layouts in simulation and deploys to ≥2 real hands, with or without calibration.
- **Note:** this is the highest-value problem for a project that wants its result to matter, and it is the hardest — real multi-hand tactile hardware is scarce (HRDexDB gets touch on only the Inspire hands; Allegro has none).

### OP5 — Few-shot transfer *of contact dynamics*, not of perception: does a policy trained on hand A learn contact strategies that survive on hand B when B's reachable contact manifold is strictly smaller?
- **Confidence it is open: ~80%.** MARC's most honest result is that the smallest hand (Wuji 12, two disabled fingers) gains the least (0.29 → 0.51, and it scores **zero** on the cookie because three fingers crumble it). DexGrasp-Zero reports that dominant failures on unseen hands come from small objects where missing touch prevents detecting partial contact. Nobody has systematically characterized the *reachable-contact-manifold* mismatch as the limiting factor, as opposed to representation mismatch.
- **Falsified by:** a study that, holding the representation fixed, varies only the target hand's contact coverage and shows transfer degrades predictably with contact-manifold deficit (or doesn't).
- **Why it's attractive:** it reframes cross-hand transfer as a mechanics problem (what grasps are even feasible?) rather than a representation problem, and it is measurable in simulation without new hardware.

### One-line takeaway for the project
The gap is real but **narrow and closing**: the substrate (Bench2Dex: 12 hands + unified tactile), the canonicalization machinery (MANO UV maps, taxel canonicalization, deform maps, functional-area tokens), and the cross-hand zero-shot policy machinery (CrossDex / DexGrasp-Zero / UHAS / XL-VLA, all non-tactile) all exist as of late 2026 — **nobody has combined them.** A credible contribution is "take Bench2Dex's tactile stream, add a canonicalization of your choice, and show the first train-on-N-hands / zero-shot-on-held-out-hand tactile policy," with MARC's calibrated-torque interface as the real-world sanity check and the IEEE Access DexPiHNet paper as the thing to read first in case it already did this.
