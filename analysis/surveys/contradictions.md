## 2. Ranked by how much resolution would change practice

1. **C2 — predicted/imagined touch: control signal or training signal?** Underwrites the entire 2026 tactile-WAM wave (TacWAM, Tactile-WAM, TacPAC, HiTac-WAM, DreamTac, VTAM, OmniVTA, TacForeSight, HapticWAM, Compact VT-WM). If Compact VT-WM generalises, most of it optimises a quantity that does not reach the controller; if HapticWAM generalises, **fingertip tactile hardware is unnecessary at deployment** — which changes product decisions for every humanoid company.
2. **C1 — tactile pollution / routing.** Immediate engineering consequence for anyone bolting touch onto a pretrained video backbone. Cheap to falsify.
3. **C3 — more/better-encoded touch hurts.** Decides sensor BOM and whether reported tactile failures are sensor faults or encoding faults.
4. **C6 — self-ablation gap + reimplemented baseline.** Not a scientific contradiction but a reporting one; it devalues the strongest positive result in the area.
5. **C4/C5 — resolution & simple baselines.** Decides hardware vs encoder spend; low urgency because both sides are weakly evidenced.

## 4. The single best contradiction to attack

**C2 — "imagined touch is enough to remove the sensor" (HapticWAM 77%, pooled 82%, beating its own tactile teacher) vs "accurate tactile forecasts need not improve force-constrained control" (Compact VT-WM: force MAE 0.413→0.338 N yet imagined RL 11.9% vs reactive 25.0%), with TacWAM's 55→37.5 collapse as the third data point.** It is the sharpest (opposite signs on the same construct), the most consequential (it decides whether tactile is a *deployment* modality or only a *training* modality), and — decisively — it is resolvable by **a controlled factorial ablation on models that already exist**, not by a new method.

**Pattern behind the negatives (Q3) — yes, and it is not "prediction is bad":**
- **Train-only** prediction supervision → neutral-to-helpful: TacWAM's masked AGT arm (82.5%), HapticWAM's distilled student (82% pooled, no sensor at test time).
- **Test-time** access of the action branch to predicted/target-derived touch → **hurts**: TacWAM Attn-AT 55→37.5, Attn-VT → 7.5%, naive VT-WAM 5.8→1.3, HapTile marker-tracked re-encoding 80→30.
- **Imagined rollouts for planning** → **hurts**: Compact VT-WM 11.9% vs reactive 25.0% (model error compounds).
The unifying hypothesis is **information routing, not prediction**: touch helps as an auxiliary training signal, and hurts the moment predicted (rather than measured) touch becomes an input the controller conditions on. That hypothesis is a publishable paper on its own, and the experiment in §3-C2 tests it directly.
