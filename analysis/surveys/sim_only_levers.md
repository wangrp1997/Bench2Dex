## 2. OCCUPIED (answers to Q1–Q6) — read Tactile Genesis first, it moved the frontier

- **Q1 resolution/sampling sweep — OCCUPIED, twice.** [Tactile Genesis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22332) ablates resolution directly and concludes "resolution matters far less than coverage; 200 whole-hand taxels suffice"; [Melnik 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/robotics-and-ai/articles/10.3389/frobt.2021.538773/full) sweeps continuous/Boolean/noisy/16-sensor; [2409.20473](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.20473) sweeps 92/62/39/29/21/14/0 on Shadow Hand + Allegro + Leap. **Do not re-run a plain resolution sweep as a headline.** Still open: resolution vs a *monitorability* metric (slip/contact-mode detectability) rather than task return, and resolution as a continuous density (taxels/cm²) *per-unit-area* rather than total count.
- **Q5 placement — OCCUPIED.** [Tactile Genesis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22332) ("placement dominates sensor type"; fingertip-only ≪ whole-hand; palm+proximal closes the gap), [2409.20473](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.20473) (per-sensor Lasso, middle finger harmful), [2509.14984](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.14984) (finger/palm distribution).
- **Q2 degradation/dropout/drift/miscalibration — MOSTLY OCCUPIED now.** [Tactile Genesis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22332) ships **drift, hysteresis, dead taxels, and crosstalk** in its noise model and ablates noise. That covers drift + dropout at the taxel level. **What remains genuinely open:** *macro* failure — whole-sensor loss, sensor-count reduction at deployment, remounting/placement shift (a sensor moved 5 mm), miscalibration of a whole finger, and graceful-degradation *curves* (success vs % taxels dead). Also open: no benchmark has a tactile perturbation axis ([Bench2Dex](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15726)'s 7 perturbations are all scene-level).
- **Q3 oracle ceiling — PARTIALLY OCCUPIED.** [Tactile Genesis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22332) reports a **privileged teacher** and measures how far ablations fall from it — that is an oracle-ceiling study in all but name. [SBLR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15897) trains on a sim oracle sensor then distills; [OVTF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00547) injects oracle futures. **What is left:** the *contact-state-specific* bound — using exact contact mode / force / object pose ground truth to bound what a tactile policy could achieve, and publishing the ceiling as a function of tactile information rather than as a single teacher baseline.
- **Q4 cross-embodiment — OCCUPIED for vision, OPEN at the intersection.** [Bench2Dex](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15726) crosses 12 hands × 26 tasks with a shared tactile interface; [DexVerse](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08751) 6 hands × 100 tasks (vision only). **But Tactile Genesis uses ONE hand (XHand1) and Bench2Dex never runs a tactile-conditioned policy.** So "does a tactile finding (e.g. placement dominates) transfer across hand morphologies?" is wide open, and it is the natural collision of the two best papers in this space.
- **Q6 information-requirement — WEAKLY OCCUPIED.** [Tactile Genesis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22332) frames the question exactly ("which tactile abstractions a policy needs and when richer tactile fields justify their hardware cost") and answers it ordinally (proprioception insufficient; F/T best; 200 taxels enough). **Not done:** a *quantitative* per-task information threshold, or an answer that generalises past 3 tasks.
- **L3 controlled counterfactuals — effectively UNOCCUPIED as a stated method.** Every work above varies factors across *separate* training runs. None pins a scene and replays it under sensor variations to attribute an outcome to sensing alone. This is the lever with the least published competition.
- **Bonus: Bench2Dex collects tactile and never uses it.** Its reported evaluation runs ACT / DP / π0.5 / GR00T N1.5 with **RGB + proprioception only** — no visuo-tactile policy is benchmarked on it.

## 3. OPEN, ranked by cost to do

1. **Cheapest — L1 macro-failure robustness channel on Bench2Dex.** Whole-sensor loss, remount/placement shift, whole-finger miscalibration, and success-vs-%dead-taxel curves, injected through the existing registry and evaluated via matched-anchor replay (L3). Tactile Genesis covers *taxel-level* noise, not *deployment-level* failure; Bench2Dex has no tactile perturbation axis at all.
2. **Cheap — L1+L3 tactile-on vs tactile-off on Bench2Dex.** The released evaluation is RGB+proprioception only, so a matched-anchor tactile ablation gives a low-variance per-task value-of-touch result. Low novelty alone, but a strong anchor section.
3. **Cheap — Q4 transfer of Tactile Genesis's findings across hands.** Re-run its placement/coverage conclusion on 2–3 Bench2Dex hands. High value: it tests the most-cited tactile-design claim against morphology, which neither paper can do alone.
4. **Medium — resolution/monitorability curve.** Success vs taxel density *and* slip/contact-mode detection AUC vs density. Tactile Genesis measured return, not observability.
5. **Medium — L2 contact-state oracle bound.** Exact contact mode/force/pose ground truth → per-task ceiling, reported as a function of tactile information. Tactile Genesis's privileged teacher is close, so this needs the finer framing to be novel.
6. **Expensive — L4 factorial task × hand × tactile-config matrix.** Bench2Dex's 12×26 cells exist but are uncrossed; filling them is a large rollout budget.

## 4. Best single sim-only unoccupied question

**"Does the tactile-design law generalise across hands, and where does sensor fidelity bound it?"** Concretely: take [Tactile Genesis's](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22332) claim that *placement dominates sensor type and 200 whole-hand taxels suffice* and test it on [Bench2Dex's](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15726) 12 hands under pinned-scene counterfactual replay, with a contact-state oracle ceiling as the reference. It sits exactly in the gap between the two strongest papers in the area — one has the tactile levers but one hand, the other has 12 hands but no tactile-conditioned policy.
Biggest prior-art risk: **[Tactile Genesis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22332) already owns the placement/resolution/noise sweep (L1), the privileged-teacher ceiling (L2), and the 20k-env scale (L4)**, and [2409.20473](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.20473) already owns per-sensor importance — so the contribution must be the **cross-morphology generalisation + the pinned-scene attribution method (L3) + monitorability**, not another single-hand sweep. Second risk: Bench2Dex supplies the harness and 12 hands, so the novelty must be the scientific curve, not the benchmark.
