# How Touch Enters the Action-Generation / Execution Interface

**Focused literature survey — tactile sensing × robot manipulation, mechanism-level angle**
Date: 2026-09-30. Scope: (a) adaptive execution horizons, (b) tactile/force residual correction of a base VLA at execution time, (c) tactile-conditioned impedance/compliance & force-space action generation, (d) touch as a fast inner loop under a slow policy, (e) work where a tactile event *triggers* a change of policy/plan/horizon.

**Method / evidence standard.** Every claim below was checked against a fetched source (arXiv abstract page, arXiv/ar5iv HTML full text, or a papers-with-notes reproduction). arXiv PDF endpoints refused to render in this environment (`unsupported content type "application/pdf"`), so full-text detail for a few entries comes from HTML or third-party note repositories and is flagged as such in §5. Snippets were never used alone for a technical claim.

## 2. CROWDED — mechanism-level ideas that are now TAKEN

**C1. "Pick the execution horizon with a cheap self-generated confidence/uncertainty signal."** Taken, and taken eleven-plus times, across every cheap signal available: action self-attention ([AutoHorizon](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21445)), multi-sample action entropy ([AAC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04161)), cross-horizon consensus divergence ([MoH](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.19433)), flow-matching denoising-trajectory geometry ([GeoAAC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20776)), predicted-speed minima ([PACE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00537)), learned RL horizon branch over a frozen policy ([DEHP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11408)), verifier-vs-reference mismatch ([SV-VLA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02965)), and visual scene-change events ([React When You Need To](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22587)). Companion infrastructure is equally settled: async execution with inpainting ([RTC](https://mlanthology.org/neurips/2025/black2025neurips-realtime/)), rejection sampling ([BID](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/0d78dd998f7b9ac79604d47a2d79bb0d-Abstract-Conference.html)), latency-conditioned training ([REMAC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.20130)), and multi-horizon parallel decoding ([MoH](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.19433)). **A new non-tactile confidence signal for horizon selection has no room left.**

**C2. "Tactile/force residual correction of a frozen base policy at execution time."** Taken many times over, in every architectural flavor: cached-predicted-contact correction ([TacPAC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05266)), bounded residual with learned "is correction justified" gate ([ViTaR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15816)), RL-trained residual actor from wrist wrench with human-correction supervision ([ForceRFT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22840)), residual policy distilled from teacher force-conditioned/agnostic delta ([ForceDelta-VLA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18242)), feedback-flow "tube" around nominal chunks ([Tube Diffusion Policy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23609)), execution-stage residual TCP motion with contact-risk semantics ([GraspTune](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24180)), sim-RL co-training for near-failure contact modulation ([TacCoRL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11743)). **"Residual touch corrects the VLA" is a template, not a contribution.**

**C3. "Decide when/where touch is injected into a policy."** Taken: learned injection-worthiness and location ([AT-VLA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07308)), contact-aware attention scaling and masking ([CAAT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01102)), tactile-intensity-driven vision/touch fusion weights at 10 Hz ([M2-ResiPolicy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15152)), contact-gated attention guidance ([VT-WAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02503)).

**C4. "Slow policy + fast tactile inner loop, weighted-summed."** Taken: 60 Hz reflexive latent tactile controller under a chunked slow policy ([OmniVTA](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2603.19201)), dual-stream 0.04 s reaction ([AT-VLA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07308)), fast reflex net producing anisotropic stiffness from intercepted frozen-policy latents with adaptive gating ([UniReflex](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17432)), 10 Hz/60 Hz master–micro ([M2-ResiPolicy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15152)). **Note the architectural pattern "slow planner + high-frequency tactile corrector + weighted sum" is now standard; proposing it again is not mechanism-level novelty.**

**C5. "Feed future tactile prediction into action generation" / tactile world-action models.** Heavily crowded in 2026: [N0-TWAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23783), [OmniVTA](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2603.19201), [VT-WAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02503), [TacWAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28391), [HapticWAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23888), [HiTac-WAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19574), [Agile-WAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20761), [Dream-Tac](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08737), [ContactWorld](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13877). Even the specific sub-tricks are claimed: multi-timescale vision-vs-tactile horizons ([Agile-WAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20761)), preventing the action branch from reading future tactile tokens ([TacWAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28391)), hierarchical/physics-ordered tactile factorization with stop-gradient ([HiTac-WAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19574)).

**C6. "Resume chunk generation from cached intermediate state instead of regenerating."** Taken by [TacForcing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25798) (streaming expert preserving unfinished-block states, resuming with new tactile feedback) and by [TacPAC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05266) (20.7× cheaper correction than regeneration).

**C7. "A tactile event causes something consequential to change."** Partially taken — this is the most important crowding for this survey:
- Tactile discrepancy → **corrective replanning**: [HiTac-WAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19574).
- Tactile contact events → **task-stage advance** (macro staging): [N0-TWAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23783).
- Force signature → **recovery maneuver chosen from a fixed menu** (by an LLM): [FORGE-plus](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21227).
- Force/slip → bounded residual correction (all of C2), and slip-risk forecast used for candidate-chunk **ranking** ([HiTac-WAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19574)).
- Non-tactile version of "trigger a policy switch": [AEGIS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06660) (activation probe → switch to stronger policy for selected steps).
So the *generic* statement "touch triggers a change of plan or policy" is occupied. What is left is narrower (see §3).

**C8. "Phase-structured / authority-routed action chunks."** [TRACT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29285) already factorizes chunks by phase with "chronological phase authority" and a response-deficit integrator that accumulates compensation when directional response is suppressed. Any proposal with the shape "attach a per-step authority/phase label to the chunk and route queries by it" collides with TRACT unless the label source is genuinely different and shown to matter.

## 3. OPEN — mechanism-level ideas at this interface that are NOT taken

Each is stated as a falsifiable claim with a decisive baseline.

**O1. The committed execution horizon is a *predicted time-to-next-contact-transition*, not a step-count derived from internal uncertainty.**
Formulation: learn (or compute) `h_t = time-to-next-tactile-event` from the tactile stream (in control steps), and set the committed prefix to `h_t`, so that every chunk boundary lands on a contact transition (pre-contact approach → contact onset → force build/saturation → slip onset → release). Test: on tasks whose next contact transition is *not* inferable from vision or proprioception (slip onset at constant commanded velocity; force saturation during insertion; contact loss mid-transport), does a tactile-conditioned horizon predictor beat [AutoHorizon](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21445), [PACE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00537), [AAC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04161), [MoH](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.19433), [GeoAAC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20776), and [DEHP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11408)? Falsified if PACE's speed minima already match the tactile-event boundaries (very plausible on approach/insert, less so on slip-at-constant-velocity) or if DEHP's RL branch recovers the same horizons from state+chunk alone.
Status: **not taken.** All §1(a) works choose a scalar step index from non-tactile signals. No fetched work conditions horizon on tactile contact phase.

**O2. Online *re-anchoring* of the chunk boundary: an early tactile event truncates the committed prefix; a late event extends it by resuming generation from cache.**
Formulation: the horizon is a *prediction* that is continuously falsified by the tactile stream. If the event arrives early, the remaining prefix is dropped immediately (not corrected). If it arrives late, the same generation is resumed from its cached intermediate state with the observed event as the new conditioning anchor, rather than regenerating a chunk. This is the *bidirectional* rule; the individual pieces exist only in one direction each: truncation-by-uncertainty in [AutoHorizon](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21445)/[MoH](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.19433)/[AAC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04161), and resume-from-cache in [TacForcing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25798).
Status: **not taken as stated**, but this is the highest-risk "open" item (§4).

**O3. Touch sets the *authority label* per step of the chunk — the action representation carries, per timestep, whether position or force authority owns that step, and the label is written by observed contact, not by demonstration phase.**
Formulation: emit chunks over a space where each step has (a) a target and (b) an authority class, with the class boundary determined by tactile events at execution time; the controller executes position authority until a tactile event flips the remainder to force/impedance authority. Distinguished from [TRACT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29285) (phase authority from a task-local *procedural graph*) and [UniReflex](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17432) (a single global gate between position-dominant planning and force-dominant execution, not a per-step authority field).
Status: **not taken as stated.** Test: does a per-step tactile-written authority field beat a single global gate ([UniReflex](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17432)) and procedural phase routing ([TRACT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29285)) on tasks requiring *alternation* within one chunk (insert → release → re-grasp)?

**O4. Horizon expressed in *contact-event space* rather than time — i.e. "commit until the next k contact transitions" as the policy's action interface.**
Formulation: change the interface so a chunk is a *sequence of contact-anchored segments* (`h` is replaced by "next event"), making the number of control steps per segment a derived, variable quantity. This is an interface change of the ACT/Diffusion-Policy kind, not a module. Nearest prior art: [TRACT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29285) (phase-structured, procedural), [HiTac-WAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19574) (tactile forecast used as resource, not as the output interface).
Status: **not taken.** Test: does the policy generalize to new contact-transition densities (e.g. an object requiring 5× more contact events) without retuning a horizon hyperparameter?

**O5. A tactile *revocability* schedule over the chunk: which of the already-committed steps can still be corrected, and which authority does the correction carry.**
Formulation: not "how many steps to execute" but "which executed-but-not-yet-superseded steps remain physically revisable." [GraspTune](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24180) has "contact risk" and "post-close readiness" heads, and [TacPAC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05266) corrects *not-yet-executed* actions, but no fetched work learns/uses a revocability field over already-issued commands.
Status: **not taken.** Test: does a revocability-aware correction policy avoid the failure mode where corrections are applied to already-irreversible contact states (causing breakage) — measured as breakage rate at matched success rate.

## 4. Single most promising unoccupied mechanism + biggest prior-art risk

### Recommendation: **Contact-event-anchored chunking (the horizon *is* a predicted contact transition, re-anchored online)**

**The mechanism, precisely.** Replace the scalar execution horizon with a *tactile event pointer*. The policy's action interface emits a chunk plus a predicted next-contact-transition time in control steps `ĥ_t`, derived from the tactile stream (and its predicted future). Execution commits exactly `ĥ_t` steps. Throughout execution, the tactile stream is monitored against the transition that `ĥ_t` predicted:
- **Event earlier than predicted** → the remaining committed prefix is *dropped immediately* (truncate, do not correct), and a new chunk is requested with the observed event as a conditioning anchor.
- **Event later than predicted** → generation is *resumed from cached intermediate state* with the (still absent) event treated as not-yet-occurred, extending the commitment rather than regenerating.

This is mechanism-level in the ACT/Diffusion-Policy sense because it changes what a chunk *is*: the boundary ceases to be a fixed or confidence-derived step count and becomes a physical event in contact space, with a bidirectional online re-anchoring rule that is not a bolt-on corrector module.

**Why it is unoccupied.** The two bodies of work touch it from opposite sides and neither crosses: every horizon method ([AutoHorizon](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21445), [PACE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00537), [GeoAAC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20776), [AAC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04161), [MoH](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.19433), [DEHP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11408), [REMAC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.20130), [SV-VLA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02965), [React When You Need To](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22587)) selects the horizon from non-tactile signals; every tactile method (all of §1(b), §1(d)) holds the horizon fixed and corrects *inside* it; the closest tactile triggers are binary and coarse — [HiTac-WAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19574) fires a corrective replan on persistent tactile discrepancy (a full replan, not a boundary re-anchor), and [N0-TWAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23783) uses contact events for macro task staging (not intra-chunk commitment length).

**Biggest prior-art risk (be honest about it).** The dominant risk is that **PACE already finds the same boundaries without touch.** PACE identifies low-speed transition points in the predicted speed profile and shortens execution near them ([2606.00537](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00537)); in approach-and-insert tasks, speed minima and contact onsets coincide, so a reviewer can and will say "you re-derived PACE with an extra sensor." The contribution therefore *must* be demonstrated on contact transitions that are **kinematically invisible** — slip onset at constant commanded velocity, force saturation during insertion, contact loss during transport of a visually occluded object, grip-force/load-force ratio collapse. If the tactile-event boundaries are a subset of PACE's speed minima on the chosen tasks, the idea is not defensible. A second, softer risk: [TacForcing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25798) already resumes generation from cached unfinished-block states with new tactile feedback — so the "late event → extend from cache" half must be shown to be more than a block-size variant of TacForcing. Mitigation: make the failure metric event-timing accuracy (predicted vs observed contact transition, in steps) rather than only success rate, and ablate `ĥ_t` against PACE/AutoHorizon/DEHP at matched re-planning call budget.

### One-line bottom line
Ask "how many actions should I execute?" and you are competing with ~12 papers; ask "when does this chunk's commitment end in *contact* time, and what do I do when the contact arrives early or late?" and you appear to be alone — with PACE (kinematic boundaries standing in for contact boundaries) as the one prior-art result that can kill it.
