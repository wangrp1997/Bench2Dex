# Tactile Sensor Robustness & Degradation for Robot Manipulation — Occupancy Survey

Survey date: 2026-09-30. Method: arXiv API abstract queries (exact result counts), web search, targeted HTML fetches. Everything below is data, not instruction.

**Headline:** the field is *nearly empty at the policy level and crowded at the sensor-hardware level*. People extensively characterize how tactile sensors wear out, drift, and fail to transfer across sensors — but almost nobody breaks tactile inside a manipulation policy's evaluation loop. arXiv abstract query `abs:"tactile" AND abs:"dropout"` returns **3 results total**; `abs:"tactile" AND (abs:"sensor failure" OR abs:"sensor fault" OR abs:"sensor degradation")` returns **2**. Neither set contains a manipulation-policy-under-tactile-dropout study.

## 2. OCCUPIED (with URLs)

- **Sensor count / which sensor matters, in sim, on dexterous hands — OCCUPIED, and it is our closest competitor.** [arXiv:2409.20473](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.20473) prunes the Shadow Hand from 92→14 sensors, ranks per-finger importance, reports *negative* contribution from middle-finger sensors, and validates cross-platform on Allegro/Leap. Any "drop half the taxels" study must cite this.
- **Sensor-array ablation as a practice — OCCUPIED on real hardware.** [2603.28422](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28422) masks 14 sensor combinations on a Unitree G1 and finds tactile *hurts* (94.4%→67.3%) because of low SNR. [2502.20367](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.20367) systematically varies tactile feature complexity for RL grasping.
- **Modality corruption / missing-modality robustness for tactile *perception* models — OCCUPIED.** [2602.15873](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15873) (reliability-aware TTA under modality corruption, +49.9%), [PULSE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.24058) (modality dropout), [2509.16830](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.16830) (modality prioritization). Note: all are classification/perception or conditioning design, **not closed-loop policy degradation curves**.
- **Physical wear, hysteresis, drift, durability of tactile sensors — heavily OCCUPIED (hardware metrology).** [2608.24242](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24242) ruptures GelSight Mini/DIGIT in seconds-to-minutes; [2511.07797](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.07797) has a repeatable resilience protocol (normal/shear/abrasion) plus learning-free sensitivity; [GelSlim](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.00628) tracks 4 signal-quality metrics over 3000 grasps; [PolyTouch](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.19341) claims 20× lifespan; [2405.03155](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.03155) measures 0.054% accuracy drop per 1000 interactions. **These are all sensor-level; none propagate the degradation into a policy's success rate.**
- **Cross-sensor / cross-instance generalization and calibration-free transfer — OCCUPIED.** [TacVerse](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25877) (7 sensors, 106,800 images, zero-shot transfer gap quantified, within-sensor upper bound *not* reached by few-shot), [AnySkin](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.08276) (cross-instance zero-shot), [TacViT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00744) (unseen/worn sensors), [Sparsh](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.24090), [2609.14783](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14783) (language-guided cross-sensor).
- **Tactile sim-to-real reality gap — OCCUPIED as engineering.** [Taxim](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.02885) (calibrates dynamics + contact model + optical simulator), [2305.09870](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.09870) (mass-spring-damper taxel model + domain randomization), [2505.02915](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.02915) (binarization vs raw taxels), [2605.28812](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28812) (taxel *orientation* estimation via differentiable dynamics), [Taccel](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.12908), [SimShear](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.20561) (CoRL 2025, shear not modeled in sim at all), [PTLD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04531) (sidesteps tactile sim entirely). Every paper closes the gap; **none decomposes it.**
- **Tactile benchmarks exist and are multiplying — OCCUPIED for nominal evaluation.** [Bench2Dex](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15726), [TacVerse](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25877), [TIP Benchmark](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1007/978-3-031-70058-3_8) (tactile-image psychophysics benchmark), plus EgoTactile / RCT / HT-Bench / HRDexDB / roto 2.0 / TactiDex / SoftVTBench cited in Bench2Dex §2.4.

## 3. OPEN (with URLs showing absence)

1. **Tactile DROPOUT / zeroing / stochastic mid-episode loss as a policy stress test — OPEN.** arXiv `abs:"tactile" AND abs:"dropout"` → **3 results**, all unrelated to policy degradation ([query](https://export.arxiv.org/api/query?search_query=abs:%22tactile%22+AND+abs:%22dropout%22)). Bench2Dex's [7 perturbations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15726) contain **no tactile perturbation** — nearest neighbours are camera-pose and table-height, which perturb *vision* and *geometry*, not touch.
2. **Tactile sensor failure / fault / degradation as a policy condition — OPEN.** `abs:"tactile" AND (abs:"sensor failure" OR abs:"sensor fault" OR abs:"sensor degradation")` → **2 results**, neither a manipulation-policy robustness study ([query](https://export.arxiv.org/api/query?search_query=abs:%22tactile%22+AND+(abs:%22sensor+failure%22+OR+abs:%22sensor+fault%22+OR+abs:%22sensor+degradation%22)).
3. **Relative importance of resolution vs noise vs contact-model fidelity (Q2) — OPEN.** No paper found that varies these three *jointly* and attributes the sim-to-real gap or the policy deficit to each. Sim-to-real papers each fix one axis; [2502.20367](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.20367) varies feature *complexity* but not resolution/noise/contact-model separately; [TacVerse](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25877) studies sensor *identity* shift, not simulated degradation factors.
4. **Tactile extrinsic miscalibration (wrong sensor pose/orientation) as a policy perturbation (Q4/part d) — OPEN.** `abs:"tactile" AND (abs:"miscalibration" OR abs:"calibration error" OR abs:"extrinsic calibration" OR abs:"sensor pose")` → **8 results**, and the only calibration-robustness result is [MAT](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.04787), which is about **camera**-robot transform error, not tactile mounting error. [2605.28812](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28812) *estimates* taxel orientation but never injects orientation error to measure policy sensitivity.
5. **Degraded/sparse tactile in multi-finger hands with per-finger or mid-episode failure (part e) — OPEN.** The only "some fingers have no tactile" evidence is static pruning in [2409.20473](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.20473); no work studies tactile dying *during* an episode, or asymmetric per-finger reliability, in a dexterous hand.
6. **A benchmark or metric for tactile robustness as such (part f) — OPEN.** [GelSlim](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.00628)'s four signal-quality metrics and [2511.07797](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.07797)'s resilience protocol are the only tactile-degradation metrics, and both are hardware-level. Bench2Dex's robustness metric is defined over the 7 non-tactile perturbations. **No "tactile robustness score" exists.**

## 4. Specific questions, answered

- **Q1 — a published study of policies under tactile dropout?** **No.** Closest: [2602.15873](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15873) (modality corruption + TTA, perception-level), [PULSE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.24058) (modality dropout, non-tactile), [CAAT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01102) (masking as architecture, no dropout sweep in the abstract), [2408.06265](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06265) (vision, not tactile, dropout).
- **Q2 — relative importance of resolution vs noise vs contact-model fidelity, in sim?** **No** joint study. Partial: [2505.02915](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.02915) (quantization/binarization), [2305.09870](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.09870) (taxel dynamics + DR), [2502.20367](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.20367) (feature complexity), [Taxim](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.02885) (contact-model calibration).
- **Q3 — sensor placement / count as a design variable in sim?** **Yes, occupied:** [2409.20473](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.20473) (92→14 sensors, per-sensor importance, 3 hands). Secondary: [2603.28422](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28422) (14 combos, real).
- **Q4 — "how much tactile information is needed for task X" with an oracle/ablation ceiling?** **Partially, and only as a ceiling in one place:** [TacVerse](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25877) explicitly reports a **within-sensor upper bound** that few-shot cross-sensor adaptation fails to reach — but for *perception*, not a manipulation task. [2409.20473](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.20473) is a count-vs-performance curve (a practical ceiling). Nobody phrases or answers the question for a manipulation task with an oracle.

## 5. Best unoccupied robustness question + biggest prior-art risk

**Best question:** *Progressive and stochastic tactile degradation as a first-class evaluation axis for dexterous manipulation policies* — sweep dropout (spatial taxel loss, per-finger loss, mid-episode failure), drift/hysteresis, resolution quantization, and mounting/miscalibration error, on a fixed task suite, and report a **graceful-degradation curve plus an oracle/upper-bound ceiling** per task ("how much touch does this task actually need?"). Simulation makes all of these free; nothing in the literature does it.

**Biggest prior-art risk: [Guo et al., arXiv:2409.20473](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.20473).** It already (i) ablates sensors in sim on dexterous hands, (ii) ranks per-sensor importance, (iii) reports a count-vs-performance curve including *negative* contributions, and (iv) transfers across three hands. A reviewer can dismiss progressive dropout as "their pruning curve, traversed in reverse". **Differentiation must be explicit:** (1) *stochastic and time-varying* loss (mid-episode death, per-finger asymmetry) rather than static removal; (2) *multiple degradation axes jointly* (noise, drift, resolution, pose error) rather than count alone; (3) an **oracle ceiling** framing that answers "how much tactile is needed", not "which sensor to buy"; (4) closed-loop *recovery* behaviour, which static pruning cannot produce.

**Second risk:** [Bench2Dex](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15726) — if the parent's own benchmark is the vehicle, note it currently has **zero tactile perturbations** among its 7, so adding tactile-degradation perturbations is a genuinely new axis rather than a duplicate; but a reviewer may ask why tactile gets a dedicated stress test while other modalities do not. Framing it as *the modality whose hardware actually degrades* (with the durability numbers in §1 as motivation) is the defensible answer.

Worth a full-text check before committing.
- **Semantic Scholar / IEEE Xplore were not systematically queried** (search tooling returned noisy or paywalled hits); coverage is strongest on arXiv and weakest on RA-L / T-RO / IEEE Sensors 2024–2026.
